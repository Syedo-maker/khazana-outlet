# Week 1: Competitor analysis

## The honest starting position

A web search for an organised Pakistani dead stock marketplace returns
nothing. That is encouraging but it is weak evidence, not proof of a gap. The
real competition in Pakistan is informal, offline and invisible to search
engines. Your job this week is to map it in person.

Treat the sections below as a starting map with what is already confirmed,
plus the specific questions you need to answer yourself.

## Competitor categories

### 1. Physical wholesale and surplus markets (the real incumbent)

Confirmed to exist and to trade exactly this kind of stock:

- Zainab Market, Karachi: export leftovers, overruns and rejects, mixed
  quality, sold to retail buyers and small resellers
- Rex Centre, Saddar, Karachi: dealers in brand export leftover garments
  selling to domestic wholesalers and overseas buyers, for example Sharrys
- Lahore: Azam Cloth Market, Anarkali, Shah Alam Market
- Faisalabad: textile overrun and reject trade

Why this matters: when a brand has leftover stock today, it most often goes
to a named dealer in one of these markets, in one truck, for cash, with no
paperwork and no brand attribution. That is a very good deal for the brand in
three ways you must be able to beat or match:

1. Instant cash, usually same day
2. One transaction, zero operational effort
3. Total anonymity, the brand name never appears anywhere

Questions to answer in week 2 and 3:
- What percentage of the original retail price does a dealer pay? Get real
  numbers from at least five brands and five dealers.
- How fast is payment, and is it cash or cheque?
- Who are the three or four largest dealers per city?

### 2. Brands' own clearance channels

- Outlet stores and factory shops
- End of season sales, both in store and online
- Permanent sale sections on brand websites
- Employee and family sales

Questions to answer:
- For each Tier B brand, does it already run outlets, and how much stock does
  that absorb?
- What is left over after their own sale, and where does it go?

### 3. Online classifieds and groups

- OLX Pakistan, with active stock lot and bulk clothing listings
- Facebook wholesale and reseller groups
- WhatsApp broadcast lists run by dealers
- Instagram resale and bulk pages

These are your closest functional competitor for the buyer side. They are
free, already have the audience, and have zero trust mechanism. Your wedge is
verification, escrow and consistent supply, not price.

Questions to answer:
- Which three groups do resellers actually buy from? Ask every reseller in
  week 3 and count the mentions.
- What goes wrong for them there? Fake goods, no recourse, advance payment
  fraud, wrong counts and sizes. Record real stories, they become your
  landing page copy and your trust features.

### 4. General marketplaces

- Daraz, including its clearance and sale events
- Brand stores on Daraz

Daraz is not a dead stock platform but it absorbs clearance volume and it
sets buyer price expectations. It is also the most likely large competitor to
copy the idea once you prove it works.

Questions to answer:
- Can a brand liquidate a 2,000 piece lot on Daraz today, and what does that
  cost in commission and effort?
- What is Daraz's commission range for your categories? Get this from a
  seller, not from a blog.

### 5. International reference models

- Queen of Raw, United States, founded 2018, marketplace for deadstock
  fabric with inventory tools that match supply and demand. Mostly fabric,
  not finished products. Correction to carry forward: it is American, not
  British, as noted in the plan document.
- B-Stock Solutions, the largest B2B liquidation marketplace globally, runs
  private auction marketplaces for large retailers.
- Via Trading, BULQ, Direct Liquidation, United States wholesale liquidation
  marketplaces selling pallets and truckloads to resellers.

What to take from them, specifically:
- B-Stock's private, invitation only auction per brand is the strongest
  existing answer to brand image fear. It is close to the private sale option
  in your plan and it is proven at scale.
- Lot level condition grading and manifest accuracy are what buyers complain
  about most on all of these platforms. Build the manifest right from day
  one.

## The one question that decides the business

A brand can already call a dealer tomorrow and get cash for the whole lot.
Why would it use a platform instead?

Only a few answers are strong enough, and you must validate which ones brands
actually care about. Rank these by how often brands raise them unprompted:

1. Better price, because many buyers compete instead of one dealer
2. Control, meaning the brand chooses who sees the stock and in which cities
3. Brand safety, meaning no product leaks into a market with its name on it
4. Data, meaning a record of what sold and at what price
5. Legitimacy, meaning a proper invoice and a clean entry in the books rather
   than an undocumented cash sale

Answer 5 is underrated and it is worth probing hard. A documented, taxable
sale channel can matter more to a finance manager than a few percent of
price, and no dealer in Zainab Market can offer it.
